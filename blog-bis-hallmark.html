BIS Hallmark Certification Explained:
How to Verify Your Gold Jewellery

Princess Jewellery Atelier 4 minute read Buyer Protection

Every year, thousands of Indian consumers unknowingly purchase impure gold jewellery. They pay for 22K but receive 18K. They pay for 18K and receive plated brass. The BIS Hallmark system was created by the Government of India to put an end to this fraud — and since 2021, it is mandatory by law for all gold jewellery sold in India. This guide will help you understand exactly what to look for before buying any piece of gold.

Important: As of June 2021, the Government of India made BIS Hallmarking with a 6-digit HUID compulsory for all gold jewellery sold in India. Buying jewellery without a valid HUID is both illegal (for the seller) and risky (for you).

What Is BIS Hallmarking?

BIS stands for Bureau of Indian Standards — India's national standards body, operating under the Ministry of Consumer Affairs. BIS hallmarking is the official process by which a government-authorized testing centre verifies that a piece of gold jewellery contains exactly the purity it claims. Once verified, the piece receives a BIS stamp and a unique ID number.

The 3 Components of a BIS Hallmark Stamp

1. The BIS Logo (Triangle with "BIS" inside)

This triangular logo confirms that the jewellery has been tested by an officially accredited BIS Assaying and Hallmarking Centre (AHC). Without this logo, the piece has NOT been officially verified — even if the seller claims otherwise.

2. The Purity Number

The purity number stamped on the jewellery tells you exactly how much gold it contains:

  • 999 = 24 Karat = 99.9% pure gold
  • 916 = 22 Karat = 91.6% pure gold
  • 750 = 18 Karat = 75.0% pure gold
  • 585 = 14 Karat = 58.5% pure gold

3. The 6-Digit HUID (Hallmark Unique Identification)

The HUID is a 6-character alphanumeric code (mix of letters and numbers) that is unique to each individual piece of jewellery. Think of it like an Aadhaar card — but for your gold. No two pieces of jewellery in India can have the same HUID. This code is laser-engraved or stamped onto the jewellery at the hallmarking centre.

How to Verify the HUID Online in 2 Minutes

The Government of India provides a free online tool to verify any HUID code. Here is how to use it:

  1. Open the BIS Care app on your smartphone (available on Android and iOS), OR visit the BIS CAAB portal at bis.gov.in
  2. Click "Verify HUID"
  3. Enter the 6-character code from the jewellery piece
  4. The portal will show you: the exact purity, the jeweller's name and location, and the hallmarking centre that tested it

If the code is not found, or if the details shown don't match what the seller told you — do not buy the piece.

At Princess Jewellery, we provide the HUID code for every single piece we sell. We encourage all our wholesale partners and retail customers to verify their purchase on the BIS portal before completing payment. We have zero-tolerance for any compromise on purity.

Red Flags — What to Watch Out For

  • The piece has no BIS triangle logo or no stamp at all
  • The purity stamp says "916" but the HUID verification shows 750
  • The seller refuses to show you the HUID or says it's "on the certificate" but not on the jewellery
  • The jewellery is priced significantly below the current market gold rate
  • The seller says "hallmarking is not required for this type of jewellery" — this is false

What About Gold Bought Abroad?

If you are purchasing gold jewellery from Dubai, Singapore, or the UK, note that BIS hallmarking only applies to jewellery sold within India. However, these countries have their own hallmarking systems (Dubai: Dubai Central Laboratory; UK: Assay Office). At Princess Jewellery, all our export pieces comply with the destination country's purity standards AND carry BIS certification for the Indian market.
