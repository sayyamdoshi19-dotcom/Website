Royal Indian Bridal Jewellery Trends
for 2026

Princess Jewellery Atelier 6 minute read Bridal Couture

The Indian bridal jewellery market in 2026 is experiencing a magnificent renaissance. After years of minimalism, brides across Mumbai, Dubai, London, and Singapore are returning to maximalist, regal gold — but with a modern sensibility. The trend is "Royal Reimagined": traditional Indian craftsmanship in silhouettes that feel wearable after the wedding day. Here is your complete guide to what is ruling bridal collections this year.

Trend 1: The Maharani Kundan-Polki Choker

The wide, multi-strand Kundan choker is the undisputed queen of 2026 bridal looks. These statement necklaces feature uncut Polki diamonds set in 22K gold with enamel work on the reverse. What makes 2026 different from previous years is the fusion element — brides are pairing these heavy Kundan chokers with minimalist diamond drop earrings, creating a balanced look that feels neither too traditional nor too contemporary.

Our Maharani Bridal Choker Necklace (available in 22K hallmark gold, 25g–150g) is crafted by master karigars using the traditional Rajasthani Kundan-setting technique. We customise each piece to the bride's skin tone and outfit colour.

Trend 2: The Return of the Statement Bali

Jhumka earrings dominated the last decade. In 2026, the Suniya Bali — a modern take on the traditional bali hoop — is the earring of the season. Brides are choosing oversized, geometric balis in polished 22K gold for the reception look, and smaller, twisted-ribbon balis for the mehendi and sangeet events.

Key styles to watch:

  • Faceted Geometric Bali — Pyramidal cut surfaces that catch light from every angle
  • Twisted Ribbon Bali — Fluid spiral design that feels modern yet traditional
  • Infinity Loop Bali — Romantic and delicate, perfect for post-wedding daily wear

View our full Suniya Bali collection to explore all 4 designs.

Trend 3: The Antique Temple Kada for the Groom

Grooms are no longer limited to a single ring. 2026 is seeing a significant rise in grooms wearing ornate gold kadas — inspired by antique temple jewellery — as part of their wedding look. Heavy, embossed kadas in 22K gold with lion-head terminals or lotus motifs are particularly popular for the main wedding ceremony, paired with a sherwani.

Our Rajputana Handcrafted Solid Kada (15g–60g, 22K) is one of our best-selling groom pieces, especially for NRI weddings in the UK and Canada.

Trend 4: The Imperial Gents Signet Ring

High-karat gold rings with sacred or heritage motifs — Trishul, Om, lion crest — have become a must-have for grooms across India. These aren't just accessories; they are heirloom pieces that carry cultural and spiritual meaning. The 2026 gents ring trend leans heavily towards:

  • Oversized face with micro-pave diamond border
  • Sacred motif embossing (Trishul, Om, Swastik)
  • Dual or triple band design
  • Matte-polished combination finishes

Trend 5: Sustainable and Recycled Gold

A growing number of modern brides — particularly those in the diaspora — are requesting jewellery made from recycled or ethically sourced gold. This trend is still emerging in India but is very strong among NRI clients in London and Toronto.
