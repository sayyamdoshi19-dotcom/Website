How to Care for & Preserve
Heirloom Gold Jewellery at Home

Princess Jewellery Atelier 4 minute read Heirloom Care

Gold is one of the most stable metals on earth — it does not rust, corrode, or tarnish the way silver or copper does. But gold jewellery can still lose its shine, collect oils and dirt, and even suffer physical damage if stored or cleaned incorrectly. This guide covers everything you need to know to keep your gold jewellery looking as brilliant as the day you bought it — for decades and even generations.

Quick tip: Remove your gold jewellery before swimming in chlorinated pools or the sea, before applying perfume or hairspray, and before any heavy physical activity. These are the three most common causes of premature damage to gold jewellery.

How to Clean Gold Jewellery at Home

The safest and most effective home cleaning method for plain gold jewellery (without gemstones) is the warm soapy water soak. Here is the step-by-step process:

Step 1 — Prepare Your Cleaning Solution

Fill a small bowl with warm (not hot) water. Add 2–3 drops of mild dish soap or baby shampoo. Do NOT use toothpaste, baking soda, bleach, or any abrasive cleaning products on gold — these scratch the surface and leave permanent marks.

Step 2 — Soak for 15–20 Minutes

Place your gold jewellery in the soapy water and let it soak for 15–20 minutes. This loosens body oils, makeup residue, and embedded dirt without any scrubbing required.

Step 3 — Gently Brush

Use a very soft toothbrush (baby toothbrush is ideal) to gently scrub the jewellery, especially in the crevices and around any settings. Do NOT press hard — a light touch is all that is needed.

Step 4 — Rinse and Dry

Rinse thoroughly under clean, lukewarm running water. Ensure all soap is removed — soap residue left on gold makes it look dull. Pat dry with a soft, lint-free cloth. Do NOT rub vigorously. Then let it air-dry completely on a dry cloth before storing.

Special Care for Different Types of Gold Jewellery

Kundan or Stone-Set Jewellery

NEVER soak Kundan, Polki, or any jewellery with stones in water. The lac (resin) used in Kundan settings will dissolve in water. For these pieces, use a barely damp cloth to wipe the metal surfaces only, and store them flat in velvet-lined boxes.

Filigree and Engraved Pieces

Intricately engraved pieces like temple kadas collect dust in the crevices. Use a very soft, dry paintbrush (artist's brush) to gently sweep out dust. The warm soapy soak method works well for plain gold filigree with no stone settings.

Chain Necklaces

Chains tangle and kink when not stored properly. Lay them flat or hang them individually. When cleaning, stretch them out fully in the soapy water and use the soft brush along the length. Rinse well, as soap tends to collect in the chain links.

How to Store Gold Jewellery Properly

  • Store pieces individually — gold scratches gold. Wrap each piece in a soft cloth or place in separate velvet pouches
  • Avoid humidity — use silica gel packets in your jewellery box to absorb moisture
  • Keep away from direct sunlight — prolonged UV exposure can cause slight discolouration in lower-karat gold alloys
  • Use velvet-lined boxes — the velvet holds pieces in place and prevents scratching
  • Lock heavy pieces separately — store kadas and heavy bangles in padded compartments so they don't press against lighter pieces

When to Take Your Gold to a Professional

Home cleaning maintains day-to-day luster. But once every 1–2 years, we recommend taking valuable pieces to a professional jeweller for:

  • Ultrasonic cleaning (removes deep-set dirt safely)
  • Rhodium replating (for white gold pieces)
  • Prong check (to ensure stone settings are secure)
  • Polishing (restores original mirror finish)
