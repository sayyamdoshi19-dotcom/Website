Understanding 24K, 22K & 18K Gold:
The Complete Buyer's Guide

Princess Jewellery Atelier 5 minute read Purity Guide

When you walk into a jewellery shop in India, you will see numbers like 24K, 22K, or 18K stamped on gold jewellery. Most buyers don't know exactly what these numbers mean — and jewellers rarely explain. This guide will give you the complete picture, so you can make a fully informed decision before spending your hard-earned money on gold.

The simple rule: The higher the karat number, the purer the gold — but also the softer the metal. The "best" karat depends entirely on what you plan to do with the jewellery.

What Does "Karat" Mean?

Pure gold in nature is measured in 24 parts. So "24 karat" means the metal is made of 24 parts gold out of 24 — that is, 100% pure gold. But 100% pure gold is extremely soft. It bends with your fingers. You cannot wear it daily without it warping or scratching. This is why jewellers mix (alloy) gold with small amounts of silver, copper, or zinc to give it strength.

24 Karat (24K) Gold — 99.9% Pure

This is the purest form of gold available commercially. 24K gold has a rich, deep yellow colour that is distinctly different from lower-karat gold. It is the gold used in investment coins, bars, and bullion. However, because it is so soft, 24K gold is not suitable for rings, earrings, or necklaces that will be worn regularly. It will scratch and deform easily.

  • Purity: 99.9%
  • BIS Hallmark stamp: 999
  • Best for: Coins, bars, investment, and very simple chains
  • Not suitable for: Rings, earrings, kadas, or any jewellery with stone settings

22 Karat (22K) Gold — 91.6% Pure

22K gold is made of 22 parts gold and 2 parts other metals (usually silver and copper). This gives it enough hardness to hold intricate designs while retaining a beautiful, rich yellow gold colour. 22K is the most popular purity for traditional Indian wedding jewellery — including necklaces, bangles, kadas, rings, and jhumka earrings. It is the sweet spot between purity and durability.

  • Purity: 91.6%
  • BIS Hallmark stamp: 916
  • Best for: Bridal jewellery, kadas, chains, necklaces, rings, jhumkas
  • Slightly more affordable than 24K because it is not 100% pure

18 Karat (18K) Gold — 75% Pure

18K gold contains 18 parts gold and 6 parts other metals. It is harder and more durable than 22K, which makes it ideal for jewellery that holds diamonds or gemstones. The diamond prongs need to be strong to hold the stones securely — 18K provides that strength. 18K gold also comes in white gold and rose gold varieties.

  • Purity: 75%
  • BIS Hallmark stamp: 750
  • Best for: Diamond jewellery, engagement rings, modern western-style designs
  • Available in yellow, white, and rose gold

How to Read a BIS Hallmark Certificate

The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) is India's government body that certifies gold purity. Since January 2021, BIS hallmarking has been mandatory for gold jewellery sold in India. When you buy hallmarked jewellery, look for a 6-digit alphanumeric code called the HUID (Hallmark Unique ID). You can verify this code on the official BIS website to confirm the exact purity of your gold.

A hallmark stamp contains: the BIS logo (triangle), the purity number (999 / 916 / 750), the jeweller's code, and the HUID. Never buy gold without seeing this hallmark.

Which Karat Should You Buy?

Here is our simple recommendation chart:

  • For investment or gifting (no daily wear): 24K gold coins or bars
  • For traditional Indian wedding jewellery: 22K — the gold standard for bridal sets, necklaces, kadas
  • For daily-wear rings or diamond jewellery: 18K — stronger, holds stones better
  • For export-quality wholesale jewellery: We supply all three purities per buyer specification

Price Difference Between Karats

The price of gold jewellery is primarily driven by two things: the weight in grams and the purity. A 24K piece will cost more per gram than a 22K piece. Additionally, the jeweller charges a "making charge" (or "wastage") for the craftsmanship.
